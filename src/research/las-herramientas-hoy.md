# The tools today, and their road to Rust

> **Snapshot from 2026-09-29, at commit `66152183`** (snes-rm2k), with steps
> 1 to 5 of the separation plan done and before the names were translated to
> English. Line counts come from `wc -l` (without tests or generated code
> where it says so); dependencies, from `grep` over the `import`s and the
> `subprocess` calls. Efforts are Hornero's estimates, not measurements.

## Summary

`tools/` holds three things:

* **five Rust projects**, already in English and with nothing of the game:
  about 20,800 lines, of which 15,400 are Ditto;
* **the converter and its layers in Python**: about 33,000 lines, 23,000 of
  them in `port/`, and two files hold half of that (`snes_convert.py` 9,550
  and `eventos.py` 5,440);
* **the runtime**, the SNES engine in assembly: about 44,000 lines in 95
  files. It is not migrated: it is what runs on the console.

The Python depends on **two libraries** —Pillow in 20 modules and numpy in
six— and on **external binaries** through `subprocess`: `ca65` and `ld65`
(cc65), `fluidsynth` and `ffmpeg`, plus our own five.

**The cheapest thing is for the bridges to disappear.** Four Python modules
are only the glue between the converter and a Rust binary that already
exists; once their caller is Rust, they become a library call:

| module | lines | becomes part of |
|---|--:|---|
| `port/comprimir.py` | 117 | `lz2` |
| `port/tiles_bin.py` | 123 | `voodoo` |
| `audio/muestras_brr.py` | 306 | `gunter` |
| `audio/snes_samples.py` | 353 | `gunter`, which already reads SoundFonts |

And two more are already generated by a binary: `audio/music_opcodes.py` and
`port/event_opcodes.py` come out of `protocol`, which can emit Rust instead
of Python.

**Ditto is the other magnet.** `rpgmaker/lcf.py` (940) reads the JSON dump
that Ditto writes, and Ditto, in Rust, already has the whole model of the
format; the RM2k rules in `rpgmaker/rm2k.py` (509) —passability, layers,
poses— are semantics of the format and fit as a Ditto library.
`rpgmaker/fork.py` (269) and `render/chipset_del_fork.py` (342) apply the
patches by hand: they belong to the converter, not to the format.

**What does not go to Voodoo:** the allocator (`port/asignador.py`, 217),
which decides which colors are lost. AGENTS.md says that Voodoo does not
decide colors and that this is not to be fixed; in Rust it has to be its own
tool, or part of the converter.

## The new tools, by size

Sized by what they cost, not by lines: **S** is one day of one agent, **M** a
few, **L** a week, **XL** several, and in parts.

| Rust tool | comes from | Python lines | size | the hard part |
|---|---|--:|:-:|---|
| common library | `comun/` (project, knobs, environment, cache, parallelism) | 550 | S | the cache key: today it hashes the `.py` files by their path |
| `rm2k-snes-build` | `ensamblado/` (banks, assembling, space, cartridges) | 1,260 | M | the bank allocation and the `ld65` `.cfg`; it still calls `ca65`/`ld65` |
| `rm2k-render` | `render/` | 1,020 | M | the autotiles; Pillow → the `image` crate |
| RM2k rules in Ditto | `rpgmaker/lcf.py`, `rm2k.py`, `event_codes.py` | 1,470 | M | `lcf.py` disappears: Ditto already has the model |
| `rm2k-snes-audio` | `audio/` without the bridges (midi, musica, sdsp, spc700, snes_music, efectos_msu, msu, techos) | 4,000 | L | `snes_music.py` (2,550) is the per-map audio package; `msu` and `techos` use `fluidsynth` and `ffmpeg` |
| the converter | `port/` without the bridges | 22,500 | XL | `eventos.py` is a bytecode compiler; `snes_convert.py` still mixes everything |

Outside this table:

* **`emulador/retro.py`** (441): the libretro core from Python. ABL's tests
  use it, and they are Python, so it stays as long as they are.
* **`ensamblado/entregar.py`**: it belongs to ABL's workflow, not to the
  suite. It leaves with ABL, or becomes a recipe.
* **`parches.py`** (762): writes and checks the lossy decisions as TOML; it
  goes with the converter.

## The dependencies, and what replaces them in Rust

| today | used by | in Rust |
|---|---|---|
| Pillow | 20 modules: `render/`, almost all of `port/`, `msu`, `techos`, `retro` | the `image` crate, or just `png` |
| numpy | `snes_convert`, `asignador`, `menus`, `snes_samples`, `msu` and `techos` | vectors; nothing really needs it |
| `ca65` + `ld65` | `ensamblar` | still called: they are the assembler |
| `fluidsynth` | `msu`, `techos` | can still be called; `rustysynth` synthesizes SoundFonts in pure Rust, and would remove a system dependency |
| `ffmpeg` | `msu`, `techos` | measuring loudness can be done with the `ebur128` crate; encoding, keep calling it |

## The order I recommend

1. **The bridges and the generated files**, because they disappear on their
   own: when their caller is Rust, they are a crate dependency. There is no
   need to migrate them first: they leave with their caller.
2. **`rm2k-snes-build`** and the common library: small, with clear inputs
   and outputs, and they are what `just cartuchos` runs.
3. **`rm2k-render`** and **the RM2k rules in Ditto**: leaves, with nothing
   of the game.
4. **The audio**: large but isolated, with its own real test (the ceilings,
   `techos`, measure the ROM's audio).
5. **The converter**, last and in parts, after splitting `snes_convert.py`
   as `workflow/tasks/todo/las-responsabilidades-de-snes-convert.md` asks.
   `eventos.py` is the most expensive piece: it is best migrated with the
   bytecode declared in `protocol`, which already generates the constants
   for both sides.

While the mix lasts, a Rust binary and the Python live side by side through
the PATH, as `ditto`, `voodoo` or `lz2` do today: each step is verified the
same way as the separation steps, with the ROMs byte-for-byte identical.
