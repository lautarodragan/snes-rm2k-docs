# The S-DD1, for the poses

> **Measured on 2026-10-11** by Carpintero. A Blurred Line from the
> `build/release` of the ports at `b499ab4` (`abl.sfc` `24dd24adb52f3187`),
> Vampires Dawn from the blocks Tijereta dumped the same day; both with the
> tools of the-advocate at `6f933ff8`. The firmware read is sd2snes at
> `cf7e21d`; the emulators, the copies of bsnes (libretro), Mesen2, snes9x and
> Mednafen 1.32.1 we read code from. It is a snapshot: the numbers are from
> that day and this file is not updated. How each number was obtained is at
> the end.

The question is taro's, on 2026-10-11, after seeing what the poses cost in
LZ2: *does the S-DD1 let us compress them without adding lag?* Tijereta had
compressed every pose as its own LZ2 stream: A Blurred Line's poses go from
752 to 305 KB, but decompressing one in the main loop costs ~13% of a frame,
and the test suite loses frames —the race runs 25% slower, eight tests go
red—. Vampires Dawn needs 3.75 MB more than the cartridge's 127 data banks,
and even with the poses in LZ2 it is ~1 MB short.

The S-DD1 is the decompression chip of Star Ocean and Street Fighter Alpha
2: a DMA that reads from the ROM gets the data already decompressed, at the
DMA's speed, with no CPU. taro also said that Star Ocean runs on his FX Pak,
so the chip is there, but that he never tried MSU-1 together with another
chip; and earlier the same day, that he would rather not add a chip while
the MSU-1 stays optional, because of the complexity. This is the numbers
for that decision.


## Summary

* **It compresses better than LZ2, and costs no CPU.** The poses go to
  33% of their size as four streams of 128 bytes —what today's DMA asks
  for—, against 42% for LZ2 per row and 40% per pose; in Vampires Dawn,
  28% against 35%. The 8 bpp backgrounds, only 2–4% better than the LZ2
  they already have.
* **A Blurred Line does not need it.** It saves ~0.63 MB, ten banks, and
  the cartridge has 41 free. Without lag, yes: the pose upload costs the
  same bytes in the vblank as today, plus one store per DMA. Map loads
  would be faster: the background goes from a median of 3.1 frames of LZ2
  to 0.2 of DMA.
* **Vampires Dawn does not fit with it either.** Using it everywhere it
  wins saves ~3.46 MB against the 3.75 it needs, before what is lost
  packing into banks and before the 82 maps still excluded. It needs the
  MSU-1 either way.
* **It does not enlarge the cartridge.** The FX Pak and Mednafen reach
  8 MB through the S-DD1, as we already do. And it takes away banks
  `$40`–`$7D`: the upper 4 MB is only reachable through four 1 MB windows
  in `$C0`–`$FF`, switched by registers. Every access to data in the upper
  half becomes a bank switch, and that is the real cost.
* **MSU-1 with S-DD1 on the FX Pak: yes, by every source I could read.** The
  S-DD1 core of the firmware instantiates the MSU-1, the firmware turns it on
  for any ROM with a `.msu` next to it, and its changelog fixes the MSU-1
  volume "on Cx4, OBC1, SuperFX, SA-1, S-DD1". There are users reporting the
  MSU-1 hack of Street Fighter Alpha 2 working on the Pro. Nobody has tried it
  with our ROM; the test that would settle it is at the end of "The
  hardware".
* **Recommendation: not now.** It saves space A Blurred Line does not need
  and is not enough for Vampires Dawn, and it changes the cartridge's map
  under the whole runtime. If later the MSU-1 route needs to shrink what it
  carries, or a game needs the poses compressed with zero CPU, this is the
  way, and the numbers are here.


## How much it saves

A compressor that works: I found no free S-DD1 compressor —not among the
emulator sources we read, not searching GitHub—, so I wrote a throwaway
one: the exact inverse of Andreas Naive's decompressor, the one
every emulator uses. It tries the sixteen headers (bitplane mode × context
bits) and keeps the shortest. To trust it, every compressed block is
decompressed with **bsnes's decompressor, untouched**, with garbage after the
block (in the ROM, another block follows), and it comes back byte for byte:
zero differences in the ~60,000 pieces below.

Each block is compressed alone, because each has to be decompressed alone:
a DMA starts the stream at its source address, and when its byte count runs
out, the next DMA starts a new one.

| Game | Class | Blocks | Raw | LZ2 | S-DD1 |

| ABL | **poses, four streams of 128** | 6,016 | 752.0 KB | 316.7 (42%) | **245.2 (33%)** |
| ABL | background tiles (8 bpp) | 113 | 1,164.8 KB | 710.1 (61%) | 690.8 (59%) |
| ABL | upper layer tiles | 52 | 40.3 KB | 30.3 (75%) | 25.9 (64%) |
| ABL | sprite bank | 122 | 749.4 KB | 161.9 (22%) | 157.8 (21%) |
| ABL | ChipSet animation | 18 | 48.2 KB | 25.2 (52%) | 28.7 (60%) |
| ABL | panoramas | 18 | 245.2 KB | 185.6 (76%) | 163.1 (67%) |
| ABL | pictures | 9 | 196.8 KB | 176.0 (89%) | 164.2 (83%) |
| VD | poses, one stream of 512 | 8,748 | 4,374.0 KB | 1,518.3 (35%) | 1,182.0 (27%) |
| VD | **poses, four streams of 128** | 34,992 | 4,374.0 KB | 1,604.3 (37%) | **1,231.8 (28%)** |
| VD | background tiles (8 bpp) | 322 | 3,196.3 KB | 2,267.6 (71%) | 2,172.0 (68%) |
| VD | upper layer tiles | 269 | 359.3 KB | 280.4 (78%) | 234.2 (65%) |
| VD | sprite bank | 334 | 2,295.3 KB | 353.4 (15%) | 377.8 (16%) |
| VD | ChipSet animation | 91 | 171.4 KB | 100.8 (59%) | 122.7 (72%) |
| VD | panoramas | 32 | 517.3 KB | 417.7 (81%) | 349.2 (67%) |
| VD | pictures | 43 | 427.7 KB | 355.2 (83%) | 307.7 (72%) |

The LZ2 column is what the cartridge carries today for the classes that
already go in LZ2 —the background, the upper layer, the sprite bank, the
animation—, and `lz2 compress --no-increasing` per block for those that go
raw. A block of the background can hold more than one stream (an 8 bpp
background goes in halves of 32 KB), and each stream counts as a block.

**Why four streams of 128.** A pose is uploaded as four DMAs of 128 bytes,
one per row of its 4×4 block of tiles, because the rows are 256 words apart
in the name grid (`vram_drain`, in `runtime/load_graphics.s` of the-advocate).
No VRAM increment mode or address remapping turns that block into one
linear transfer, so with the S-DD1 each row is its own stream: they cost
1–3 points against one stream per pose, and three bytes per pose to find
where rows 2–4 start. The one-stream row is what it would be through a
detour by the WRAM (`$2180`), at twice the bytes in the vblank.

**Where it loses.** The sprite bank and the ChipSet animation compress
better in LZ2: they are long runs of repeated tiles, and LZ2 copies
them while the S-DD1 only predicts bits. Those would stay in LZ2.

**The headers chosen** say something about the data: the 4 bpp poses mostly
pick the 2 bpp mode (`$00`), not the 4 bpp one; the 8 bpp backgrounds pick
the 8 bpp mode (`$40`) or `$70` almost always.

### What it adds up to

**A Blurred Line**, with the S-DD1 wherever it beats what there is:

| | Saved |
|---|---:|
| poses (four streams, plus three bytes per pose) | 502.4 KB |
| panoramas, against raw | 82.1 KB |
| pictures, against raw | 32.6 KB |
| background, against its LZ2 | 19.3 KB |
| upper layer, against its LZ2 | 4.4 KB |
| **total** | **~641 KB, ten banks** |

Today it uses 86 of the 127 data banks (`rm2k-snes-space`), 23 of them in
the slow 4 MB: it does not need the space.

**Vampires Dawn**, the same way: poses 3,116.6 KB, panoramas 168.1,
pictures 120.0, background 95.6, upper layer 46.2: **~3,546 KB, 3.46 MB**,
against the 3.75 MB it is short. Before what packing loses and before the
82 excluded maps, it is still ~0.3 MB short. The LZ2 route Tijereta tried
gets 2.79 MB from the poses; the S-DD1 gets 0.26 MB more there, and the rest
from the classes LZ2 barely touches. Of what Tijereta measured that is not
S-DD1, only the "chunky" background (−0.39 MB) would still stack, at the
cost of decompressing on the CPU and re-planarizing.

### What it costs in time

**The poses, in the vblank: the same as today.** A DMA moves a byte every 8
master cycles whether it comes raw or from the S-DD1, and the pose is still
512 bytes in four DMAs. What it adds per DMA is writing the channel's bit in
`$4801`, which the chip clears after each transfer; if the queue stores the
four row addresses when the pose is queued (in the main loop, where there
is time), the drain only adds that store. The entry of `vq` grows from 5 to
11 bytes. Nothing in the main loop: no decompression, no ring in the WRAM.

**The backgrounds, at load**, with the model the converter uses for LZ2 (700
master cycles per command and 60 per byte) against 8 per byte of DMA:

| | LZ2 on the CPU, median | max | S-DD1 by DMA, median | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ABL, 112 backgrounds | 3.08 frames | 11.58 | 0.21 | 0.74 |
| VD, 320 backgrounds | 2.79 frames | 11.63 | 0.19 | 0.78 |

About 50 ms less per map change in the median, 180 ms in the worst one.


## The map of the cartridge

What the S-DD1 maps, read in the four emulators and in the FX Pak's core:

* **`$C0`–`$FF`: four 1 MB windows**, HiROM style, each pointing to a 1 MB
  page of the ROM chosen by a register: `$4804` for `$C0`–`$CF`, `$4805`
  for `$D0`–`$DF`, `$4806` and `$4807` for the other two. At power-on they
  point to pages 0–3. **Decompression only happens here**: a DMA has to
  read from `$C0`–`$FF`.
* **`$00`–`$3F` and `$80`–`$BF`, `$8000`–`$FFFF`: LoROM over the first
  2 MB** of the ROM, where the vectors live. Bit 7 of `$4805`/`$4807` makes
  `$20`–`$3F`/`$A0`–`$BF` mirror the first megabyte (bsnes, Mesen2).
* **`$40`–`$7D`: nothing.** Neither bsnes, Mesen2 nor the FX Pak map ROM
  there (snes9x mirrors something at `$60`–`$7F`, which nothing should rely
  on).
* **Save RAM at `$70`–`$73:0000`–`$7FFF` and at `$6000`–`$7FFF` of the
  LoROM banks**. `$70:0000` and `$A0:6000` are the addresses bsnes,
  Mesen2, snes9x and the FX Pak all agree on; ours is at `$20:6000`
  today.
* **Registers at `$4800`–`$4807`**: `$4800` and `$4801` enable
  decompression per DMA channel (the second clears itself after the DMA),
  `$4804`–`$4807` are the windows.
* **The header** goes in the LoROM position (`$7FC0` of the ROM), map mode
  `$22` or `$32`, cartridge type `$43` (no RAM) or `$45` (RAM and battery):
  that is what bsnes, snes9x and the FX Pak firmware look for.

**How many MB.** The bank registers hold four bits in bsnes, Mesen2 and the
snes9x we read —16 MB—, but **three in Mednafen and in the FX Pak core**:
8 MB, the same as our ExHiROM. The real chips only ever had 4 and 6 MB
behind them. So the S-DD1 does not make room; it only compresses.

**Does it live with our ExHiROM of 8 MB?** Not as it is; it replaces it, and
this is the cost that matters:

* **The upper 4 MB is only reachable through the windows.** Today the
  runtime reads anything with its flat 24-bit address, and the packer
  counts on the bank after one bank being the next one (the
  [ExHiROM page](../cartridge/exhirom.md)). With the S-DD1 a datum in the
  upper half is a page number and an address in a window, and someone has
  to switch the window before reading it. The NMI uploads poses in the
  vblank, so whatever it reads has to be in a window nobody switches, or
  the NMI has to save and restore it. A workable scheme: `$C0` fixed on
  page 0 and the poses, the music and everything read from the NMI in
  fixed windows; one window switched per map, with the map's data packed
  into its pages. That is a new constraint for the packer and a new layer
  between the converter's addresses and the runtime.
* **The code.** Banks 0 and 1 of the runtime are LoROM-shaped already, and
  `$00:8000` keeps mapping to the start of the ROM. But the fast mirror
  `$80`–`$BF`: in the emulators it mirrors `$00`–`$3F`, and in the FX Pak
  core, as I read the VHDL, the LoROM address keeps bit 23, so with an 8 MB
  ROM `$80:8000` reads 4 MB further on. The build already writes the code
  banks twice for ExHiROM; it would have to write them where the FX Pak
  looks too. Read, not tried.
* **The save RAM moves** to `$70:0000` or `$A0:6000`.
* **The poses**: four streams per pose, three bytes to find them, the DMA
  with its source fixed (bit 3 of `DMAPx`: bsnes and Mesen2 compare every
  read with the starting address, and snes9x only decompresses a fixed-source
  DMA), and the channel bit in `$4801` before each `MDMAEN`.


## The DMA

* **Registers**: the channel's bit in `$4800` once, and in `$4801` before
  each DMA; the source address in `$43x2`–`$43x4` pointing at the start of
  the stream in `$C0`–`$FF`, fixed (`DMAPx` bit 3); the size, the number of
  **decompressed** bytes. The chip learns the address and the size by
  listening to the writes to `$43xx`, and the start by the write to
  `$420B`.
* **Any of the eight channels** can be the one, and more than one can be
  enabled; one stream per DMA.
* **One byte per DMA cycle**: the same 8 master cycles per byte as a raw
  ROM read, so the vblank's budget for poses is the same as today. In the
  real chip that is the design —Star Ocean streams its graphics this way—;
  the FX Pak core feeds it from a prefetch FIFO.
* **HDMA**: the S-DD1 is armed by a write to `$420B` (general DMA) and only
  answers the address of an armed channel, so an HDMA table is read as
  plain ROM. Our poses go up in the vblank, when there is no HDMA. And the
  S-DD1 **does not lift** the rule that forbids a general DMA outside the
  vblank, because that one is the CPU's (the first revision of the 5A22
  hangs when an HDMA starts during a DMA; see
  `battle_animation_frames.s`): decompressing in the main loop by DMA would
  still be forbidden. The S-DD1 helps because it makes the decompression
  fit **inside** the upload that already happens in the vblank.


## The hardware

**The FX Pak runs the S-DD1**: the firmware picks its own FPGA core when
the header says map `$22`/`$32` and type `$43`/`$45` and the ROM is under
12 MB. It came in firmware v1.10.0, and v1.10.2 fixed the mirroring that
made Star Ocean work on the Pro.

**MSU-1 together with it**, from the firmware's sources:

* The S-DD1 core instantiates the same MSU-1 module as the base core, and
  maps it at `$2000`–`$2007` behind a feature bit.
* The firmware sets that bit for **any** ROM that has a `.msu` next to it,
  after choosing the core, without looking at which chip it has.
* The changelog of v1.10.2 has "apply volume boost after FPGA
  reconfiguration (fixes MSU1 volume boost on Cx4, OBC1, SuperFX, SA-1,
  S-DD1)": a bug in MSU-1 audio on those cores, so it plays on them.

**And from the people who play them**, on Zeldix, without downloading
anything:

* The [alphabetical list of MSU-1 hacks](https://www.zeldix.net/t2684-alphabetical-list-every-snes-msu1-hack)
  has one for each S-DD1 game, and also for SA-1 (Super Mario RPG, Kirby
  Super Star, Kirby's Dream Land 3) and SuperFX games (Star Fox, Yoshi's
  Island).
* [Street Fighter Alpha 2](https://www.zeldix.net/t1831-street-fighter-alpha-2),
  by gizaha (2020–2022): in April 2020 one user reports it working on an
  sd2snes Pro, and another says it works on both the original and the Pro
  because both support S-DD1 with MSU-1, and that the one combination the
  original lacks is MSU-1 with SA-1. The thread does not say whether the
  hack still uses the chip or moves the data uncompressed; its changelog
  mentions a "faster decompressor" for some data, which suggests some of
  it no longer goes through the S-DD1.
* [Star Ocean](https://www.zeldix.net/t2105-star-ocean), by Conn
  (2020): its version 8 adds a wait after the MSU-1 resume command
  because on the sd2snes it takes time, so it was being run there; nobody
  in the thread reports the hardware explicitly, and it does not say
  whether the hack keeps the chip.
* [Super Mario RPG](https://www.zeldix.net/t1811-super-mario-rpg-legend-of-the-seven-stars),
  for reference: in 2018 the sd2snes had no MSU-1 with the SA-1 (FPGA
  space), and the game fell back to its own music. The SFA2 thread says
  the Pro does have it.

So: by the sources and by the users, yes. **What nobody has tried is an 8
MB S-DD1 ROM with MSU-1 data**, ours. The test that settles it, a small ROM
taro runs on the FX Pak with a `.msu` and one `.pcm` next to it:

1. header S-DD1 with RAM (`$32`, `$45`), 8 MB;
2. for each of the eight pages, map it to `$F0` with `$4807` and
   decompress a known stream by DMA in the vblank, to the VRAM and to the
   WRAM through `$2180`; compare a checksum with the one in the ROM;
3. read the MSU-1's id at `$2002`–`$2007`, seek its data port to a known
   offset and compare what `$2001` returns, and play the track;
4. compare `$80:8000` with `$00:8000`, and write and read back `$70:0000`;
5. paint the screen green or red per check.

It would also say whether the FX Pak keeps up with a 512-byte decompressed
DMA in the vblank, which the emulators do not model.

**The emulators we use**: bsnes, Mesen2, snes9x and Mednafen all emulate the
S-DD1, and all four load the MSU-1 from the `.msu` file whatever the board,
so the combination runs in all of them. They differ in what was said: 16 MB
in bsnes, Mesen2 and snes9x, 8 in Mednafen; and snes9x only decompresses a
DMA with a fixed source.


## What it would cost, and what I recommend

**What it buys**: compressed poses with no CPU and no lag (the question),
~0.63 MB in A Blurred Line, ~3.46 MB in Vampires Dawn, and map loads a few
frames shorter.

**What it costs**:

* A second mapper, or replacing ExHiROM for every game: the packer works
  with 1 MB windows and pages per map, and the runtime switches windows
  before reading the upper half; the NMI reads only from fixed windows.
* A new codec in the converter —a compressor of our own, because there is
  no free one— and a check like `lz2`'s that it comes back equal.
* The poses as four streams with their offsets, a fixed-source DMA and the
  `$4801` store.
* Every game on real hardware needs the FX Pak, and with MSU-1, an untried
  combination for us.
* Tests on two cores for something only one device implements in hardware.

**What I recommend: not now.** A Blurred Line does not need the space, and
its poses are not compressed today, so there is no lag to remove. Vampires
Dawn does not fit with it, so it needs the MSU-1 either way, and once the
MSU-1 is mandatory there, the room the S-DD1 makes is not the bottleneck.
The day something does need it —a game whose poses have to be
compressed with zero CPU, or shrinking what goes by the MSU-1— the cheapest
first step is the hardware test above, before writing any of the rest.


## How it was measured

* **The blocks**: A Blurred Line's from its `mapdata.inc`, with the script
  that cuts every labelled block into a file; Vampires Dawn's, the same
  files Tijereta cut for its LZ2 measurement (the task
  [`vampires-dawn-entra-comprimido`](https://github.com/lautarodragan/the-advocate-work/blob/main/tasks/vampires-dawn-entra-comprimido.md), in the-advocate-work). The poses are raw; the classes that go
  in LZ2 were decompressed, splitting the blocks that hold more than one
  stream.
* **LZ2**: `lz2 compress --no-increasing`, one block at a time; for what
  already goes in LZ2, the bytes it has in the cartridge.
* **S-DD1**: the throwaway compressor, the sixteen headers per block, the
  shortest; every block back through bsnes's `decompressor.cpp`, included
  untouched.
* **The load time**: the commands and bytes of every LZ2 stream of the
  background, at 700 master cycles per command and 60 per byte, against 8
  per byte; a frame of 1,364 × 262 master cycles.
* **The space**: `rm2k-snes-space` on `abl.sfc`.
* **The map, the DMA and the hardware**: read in bsnes
  (`sfc/coprocessor/sdd1/`, `boards.bml`, `heuristics/super-famicom.cpp`),
  Mesen2 (`Core/SNES/Coprocessors/SDD1/`, `SnesConsole.cpp`), snes9x
  (`sdd1.c`, `dma.cpp`, `memmap.cpp`), Mednafen (`snes_faust/cart/sdd1.cpp`,
  `snes_faust/snes.cpp`), the sd2snes firmware (`src/smc.c`,
  `src/memory.c`, `src/msu1.c`, `verilog/sd2snes_sdd1/`, `CHANGELOG`) and
  fullsnes.
